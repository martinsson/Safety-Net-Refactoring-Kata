# The giant-vegetable classes of the Upper Wexcombe Summer Fair

## Purpose

Every year at the end of the summer, the Upper Wexcombe Summer Fair holds a show of giant
vegetables. Growers bring their best exhibit to the marquee, the stewards measure it, a panel of
judges gives marks, and the show committee publishes a results board for each class. The
judging program turns the entry cards of one show into that results board.

## Vocabulary

- **Class**: a competition for one kind of vegetable. The show currently has four classes,
  always published in this order: pumpkins (`PUMPKIN`), marrows (`MARROW`), onions (`ONION`) and
  carrots (`CARROT`).
- **Entry card**: one exhibit entered by one grower in one class. It carries a card number, the
  grower's name, the class code, the stewards' measurements (weight in kg, length and girth in
  cm), the judges' marks (one mark out of 10 per judge), the number of blemishes found, a
  symmetry reading between 0 and 1 from the measuring board, the date the card was registered,
  the grower's age and whether the grower says it is their first show.
- **Closing date**: entries close on 31 August of the show year. A short grace period is
  tolerated, at a cost.
- **Results board**: for each class, the ranked exhibits, then the side prize if any, then the
  disqualified cards.
- **Pot**: the prize money of a class, made of the entry fees of that class.
- **Cup**: each class has a challenge cup, held for a year by the class winner.
- **Certificate**: every exhibit that wins prize money receives a numbered certificate.
- **Junior**: a grower under 16.

## Main rules

**Who is judged.** Only cards whose class code is one of the four codes above are judged; other
cards are not part of the show. A card is disqualified (it stays on the board with rank 0, no
score and the reason in the note) when it was registered too long after the closing date, when
it is not registered yet, when two judges or more gave it a zero, or when the exhibit is below
the minimum size of its class (pumpkins have a minimum weight, carrots a minimum length).

**Scoring.** Each class has its own scoring formula, agreed by the committee over the years.
All formulas combine the size of the exhibit (what counts depends on the vegetable: weight for
pumpkins and onions, length for carrots, both for marrows), the judges' marks and a deduction
for blemishes. When the judges strongly disagree, the extreme marks are set aside. Juniors
receive a bonus. Some classes penalise exhibits that are suspiciously perfect in shape. Late
cards (within the grace period) lose a share of their score, and growers at their first show
receive a small encouragement, once per show year. The committee has also taken a few
decisions over the years that apply to particular seasons or growers. Scores are rounded to one
decimal.

**Ranking.** Exhibits are ranked by score, then by the judges' average. Exhibits with the same
score and the same judges' average share the same rank, and the next rank is skipped (1, 1, 3).
Among exhibits sharing a rank, the board lists the heavier exhibit first, then the one
registered earlier.

**Prize money.** Each card pays an entry fee into its class's pot (juniors pay less); the pot
has a minimum and a maximum. The first three places receive half, three tenths and one fifth of
the pot. Exhibits that share a rank share the money of the places they occupy. Amounts are paid
in multiples of 50p, and no single prize may exceed the class's maximum prize. Exhibits that
score under 10 points win nothing.

**Cups, awards and side prizes.** The winner of each class holds its cup for the year; a grower
who wins the same cup three years in a row wins it outright, which the board notes. In each
class, the best placed junior who did not win prize money receives a junior award, and the
next exhibit in line for a prize is named reserve. When at least three exhibits are judged in a
class, the heaviest exhibit (the longest, for carrots) receives a side prize of 10, shown on
its own line after the ranking.

**Season memory.** The program remembers, for the whole season, which growers have already
been judged (for the first-show encouragement) and who held each cup in previous years (for
the outright win). Certificate numbers follow on from one show to the next.
